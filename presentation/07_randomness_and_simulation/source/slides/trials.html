This picture unpacks the Monte Carlo cell. Each column is one trial, one row of the dice array; these are the first 12 rows produced by the notebook's own code with seed 7. Summing along axis 1 collapses the two dice into one total per trial. Comparing with 7 gives the event mask: only trials 7 and 11 hit 7, shown in blue and orange. Read as numbers, the mask is ones and zeros, so averaging it over all 50,000 columns gives the fraction of hits, 0.16748, close to the exact 6/36 and within about one standard error of 0.0017. Note that in these 12 trials the hit rate is 2 out of 12; small samples wobble a lot, which is exactly what the Try it with 500 trials shows.
