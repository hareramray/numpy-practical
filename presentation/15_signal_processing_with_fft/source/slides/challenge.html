Create the noise with a seeded generator, for example np.random.default_rng(0).normal(0, 0.3, size), so your results are reproducible. RMSE is the square root of the mean squared difference from the clean sine. Before filtering, the RMSE is roughly the noise level; after zeroing bins above a cutoff just above the sine's frequency, most of the broadband noise is removed and the RMSE should drop, while the sine survives. A cutoff below the sine's frequency would remove the signal itself, and a very high cutoff keeps most of the noise, so the right cutoff depends on where the useful frequencies are.
