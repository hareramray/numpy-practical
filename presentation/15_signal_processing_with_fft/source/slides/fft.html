For a real-valued signal, np.fft.rfft returns one complex coefficient per nonnegative frequency; the negative frequencies would only mirror them. np.fft.rfftfreq gives the frequency of each coefficient when you pass the sample spacing d = 1 / fs, and the spacing between bins is fs / N, here exactly 1 Hz. The magnitude divided by N, with every bin except 0 Hz and the Nyquist bin doubled because each stands for a positive and a negative frequency, recovers the amplitudes of the sines: 1 at 5 Hz and 0.4 at 40 Hz. The record holds whole cycles of both sines, so each lands exactly on one bin. Try it: use 5.5 Hz instead of 5 Hz. Now 5.5 falls between bins, and its energy leaks into nearby bins, so the spike becomes a smaller, wider bump; windowing is the usual remedy.
