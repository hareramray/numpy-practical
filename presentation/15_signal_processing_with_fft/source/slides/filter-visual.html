On the left is the spectrum as the filter sees it. The dashed line marks the 10 Hz cutoff, and everything in the shaded region to its right is set to zero: the 40 Hz spike, drawn dashed, drops to the orange dot on the axis, while the 5 Hz spike, in blue, is untouched. On the right is the notebook's third plot: the inverse transform of that edited spectrum, in orange, over the original signal in faint blue. The ripple has vanished and only the slow wave remains, exactly the pure 5 Hz sine the assert compares against. This works so cleanly because the two components sit on exact bins far apart; with noise or frequencies between bins, the result is approximate.
