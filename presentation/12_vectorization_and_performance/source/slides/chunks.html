The top strip is the input, split into the slices the loop visits. Each block starts at a multiple of 2048; the orange block is the one currently being processed, and the dashed final block is shorter because 20,000 is not a multiple of 2048. The two bars below compare the memory the temporaries need, drawn to the same scale from the notebook's printed values: a full-size temporary costs 160000 bytes, while one chunk's difference never exceeds 16384 bytes, roughly a tenth. The result is the same within floating-point tolerance. The same pattern scales to data that does not fit in memory, for example combined with the memory-mapped arrays from notebook 11. Pick a chunk size large enough that per-chunk overhead stays small, and small enough that the temporaries fit comfortably.
