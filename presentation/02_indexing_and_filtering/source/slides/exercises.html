Exercise 1

All rows, last two columns of grid

grid = np.arange(20).reshape(4, 5). Expected: [[3, 4], [8, 9], [13, 14], [18, 19]].

last_columns = grid[:, -2:]

Exercise 2

Prices from 100 to 300, both included

prices = [80, 100, 150, 300, 450]. Expected: [100, 150, 300].

affordable = prices[
    (prices >= 100) & (prices <= 300)]

Let the audience attempt both before revealing the code, which matches the reference solutions. In Exercise 1 the first colon keeps every row, and the slice -2: selects the final two columns while preserving both axes, so the result is 4 by 2. Writing grid[:, 3:] works too. In Exercise 2 each comparison builds a mask, and & keeps their intersection; using >= and <= keeps both endpoints, 100 and 300, and rejects 80 and 450. Forgetting the parentheses, or writing and instead of &, raises an error.
