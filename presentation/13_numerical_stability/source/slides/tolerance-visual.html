This slide shows why exact equality is the wrong question. Python prints the shortest decimal that identifies each double: 0.3 prints as 0.3, but the sum needs seventeen decimals, ending in a 4, so the two are different numbers. The tolerance test instead asks whether the gap is no larger than atol plus rtol times the expected value, which in this example is about 3e-13, far larger than the gap. Machine epsilon, printed by np.finfo, is the spacing between 1 and the next representable number: about 1e-7 for float32 and 2e-16 for float64, roughly 7 and 16 significant digits. Pick rtol a little above the precision your computation can actually deliver.
