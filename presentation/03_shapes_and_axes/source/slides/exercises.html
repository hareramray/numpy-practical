One reshape, one reduction

Exercise 1

24 readings as 2 days × 3 sensors × 4 measurements

readings = np.arange(24). Expected: shape (2, 3, 4), and cube.ravel() gives readings back in order.

cube = readings.reshape(2, 3, 4)

Exercise 2

One total per row, keeping shape (3, 1)

counts = [[1, 2], [3, 4], [5, 6]]. Expected: [[3], [7], [11]].

row_totals = counts.sum(axis=1,
                        keepdims=True)

Let the audience attempt both before revealing the code, which matches the reference solutions. In Exercise 1 the dimensions multiply to 2 times 3 times 4, 24, matching the number of readings, so reshape only changes how they are interpreted; the reference prints two blocks, the first running 0 to 11 and the second 12 to 23. In Exercise 2, reducing axis 1 adds the columns within each row, 1+2, 3+4 and 5+6, and keepdims=True retains the reduced axis with length 1, so the shape is (3, 1) instead of (3,). Without keepdims the check on shape fails even though the numbers are right.
