Step 2 · Transpose and reductions

A reduction collapses one axis

points = np.array([[1, 2, 3], [4, 5, 6]])
print("Per-column totals:", points.sum(axis=0))
print("Per-row totals:", points.sum(axis=1))
print("Keep the reduced axis:",
      points.sum(axis=1, keepdims=True).shape)

Per-column totals: [5 7 9]
Per-row totals: [ 6 15]
Keep the reduced axis: (2, 1)

axis=0

Adds down the rows: one total per column, shape (3,).

axis=1

Adds across the columns: one total per row, shape (2,).

keepdims=True

Keeps the collapsed axis with length 1: (2, 1).

A reduction such as sum, mean or max combines many values into fewer. The axis argument names the axis that gets collapsed, not the one that survives. For shape (students, subjects), summing axis 0 combines all students, leaving one result per subject; summing axis 1 combines all subjects, leaving one result per student. Here (2, 3) summed over axis 0 becomes (3,), and over axis 1 becomes (2,). keepdims=True leaves the collapsed axis in place with length 1, giving (2, 1), which is handy when you later divide each row by its own total. The notebook checks points.sum(axis=0) against [5, 7, 9] with np.testing.assert_array_equal.
