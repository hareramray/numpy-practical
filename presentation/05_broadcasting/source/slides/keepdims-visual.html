The row means, 5.5, 11.5 and 17.5, are the averages of each row: for example (1 + 10) / 2 = 5.5. Thanks to keepdims=True they form a (3, 1) column, so the dashed copies show each mean reused across both columns of its own row, and the blue result matches the notebook output exactly. The card on the right is the Try it: without keepdims the means are a flat (3,) array. Aligned from the right, its 3 meets the data's 2, neither is 1, and NumPy refuses. Column centering does not have this problem, because a (2,) array of column means already aligns with the last axis; keepdims there is simply clearer.
