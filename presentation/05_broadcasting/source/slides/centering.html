Step 2 · Center columns and rows

Subtract the mean, keep the axis

data = np.array([[1., 10.], [3., 20.], [5., 30.]])
centered_columns = data - data.mean(axis=0, keepdims=True)
centered_rows = data - data.mean(axis=1, keepdims=True)
print(centered_columns)
print(centered_rows)

axis=0: per column

Means have shape (1, 2) and stretch down the rows.

axis=1: per row

Means have shape (3, 1) and stretch across the columns.

keepdims=True

Keeps the reduced axis as length 1, so alignment is explicit.

Centering means subtracting a mean so the result averages to zero. Column means, axis=0, are 3 and 20; subtracting them centres each feature, giving the first printed table. Row means, axis=1, are 5.5, 11.5 and 17.5; subtracting them gives the second table, where every row is now symmetric around zero. keepdims=True leaves the reduced axis in place with length 1, so the means come back as (1, 2) or (3, 1) and line up with the (3, 2) data. The notebook then asserts that the new column and row means are zero within 1e-12. Try it: remove keepdims from the row calculation. The row means become shape (3,), which aligns against the last axis of length 2, and the subtraction fails. That is the next slide.
