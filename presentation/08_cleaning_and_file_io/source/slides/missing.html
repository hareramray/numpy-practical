Step 1 · Missing is not zero

Find what is missing or invalid

raw = np.array([[10., np.nan], [12., 20.], [np.inf, 22.]])
cleaned = raw.copy()
cleaned[~np.isfinite(cleaned)] = np.nan
print("Missing mask:\n", np.isnan(cleaned))
print("Column means ignoring missing:",
      np.nanmean(cleaned, axis=0))
assert np.isnan(cleaned).sum() == 2

NaN, not a number, marks a missing or undefined floating-point value. It has a strange property: NaN does not equal itself, so value == np.nan is always False and you must use np.isnan instead. np.isfinite is stricter still: it is False for NaN and for plus or minus infinity. Here we decide, as a data policy rather than a universal rule, that infinity is invalid too, so we copy the data and turn every non-finite entry into NaN. Then np.nanmean computes column means while skipping the holes: 10 and 12 give 11, 20 and 22 give 21. Try it: count missing entries by row and by column with np.isnan(cleaned).sum(axis=1) and sum(axis=0). You should get [1 0 1] for rows and [1 1] for columns.
