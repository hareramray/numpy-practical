Here np.where is drawn as a merge of two sources. The dashed cells are the missing mask from the previous slide. The blue row holds the two column means, which broadcasts down all three rows. For each cell, np.where asks: is this cell missing? If yes, it takes the mean of that cell's column, shown in orange; if not, it keeps the observed value. So the NaN in column 1 becomes 21 and the NaN in column 0 becomes 11, exactly the printed output. The two cards answer the Try it. The column means are unchanged, which the notebook asserts. The standard deviation, computed ignoring NaN before and on the filled array after, drops from 1.0 to 0.8165 in each column, because each filled value sits exactly at the mean and adds no spread. That is why the policy must be stated: it quietly makes data look more certain.
