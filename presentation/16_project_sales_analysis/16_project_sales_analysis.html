This is the first of two capstone projects. You manage three stores selling four products over 30 days, and you build a small report: revenue per store, the top product and the daily trend. Nothing here is new syntax. The project combines cleaning, broadcasting, aggregation and plotting from earlier notebooks into one pipeline. The cube at right is the data: days stacked as layers, each a store by product grid, with the two missing counts in orange. The dataset is synthetic and prices are fixed, so treat findings as practice, not business truth.

Step 1 · Generate the data

Reproducible synthetic sales

rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
stores = np.array(["North", "Central", "South"])
products = np.array(["Tea", "Coffee",
                    "Juice", "Water"])
prices = np.array([20., 40., 35., 15.])
units = rng.poisson(lam=np.array([12, 8, 6, 15]),
    size=(30, 3, 4)).astype(float)
units[2, 0, 1] = np.nan
units[10, 2, 3] = np.nan
print("Shape:", units.shape,
      "missing:", np.isnan(units).sum())

Shape: (30, 3, 4) missing: 2

A seeded generator

Seed 2026 gives everyone the same month of sales.

One rate per product

lam broadcasts along the last axis: about 12, 8, 6 and 15 units a day.

NaN is not zero

It marks a count we never observed. astype(float) makes room for it.

Step 2 · Clean

Impute, but keep the audit trail

Guard first

A pair with no observed day has no mean to borrow, so fail loudly.

Fill per pair

nanmean over days, one average per store and product.

Measure the guess

The mask selects exactly the revenue we estimated.

missing = np.isnan(units)
if np.any(missing.all(axis=0)):
    raise ValueError("A store/product pair "
            "has no observed counts")
mean_units = np.nanmean(units, axis=0,
                        keepdims=True)
filled_units = np.where(missing, mean_units,
                        units)
revenue = filled_units * prices
print("Revenue from imputed entries:",
      revenue[missing].sum().round(2))

Revenue from imputed entries: 584.66

6

The cleaning policy: fill a missing count with that store and product pair's average observed count across days. The result is a fractional estimated count, so anything built on it must be reported as an estimate. Notice we never overwrite units: the original array and the missing mask stay available for auditing, and revenue[missing] tells us exactly how much revenue rests on guesses, 584.66 here. Try it: compare the total with np.nansum(units * prices), which simply drops the two missing entries. That total is lower by the imputed revenue, because dropping silently treats the unknown counts as zero sales.

7

This diagram explains why the one-line fill works. nanmean over axis 0 ignores the NaN and averages the 29 observed days for each of the 12 store and product pairs. keepdims=True keeps that collapsed axis as length 1, giving shape (1, 3, 4) instead of (3, 4). np.where then broadcasts the three inputs together: the (30, 3, 4) mask, the (1, 3, 4) means stretched across all 30 days, and the original counts. Without keepdims the shapes would still broadcast here, because (3, 4) aligns from the right, but keepdims makes the intent explicit and stays correct if you reduce a middle axis instead.

Revenue is units times price for every day, store and product. Because prices has shape (4,), broadcasting aligns it with the last axis of the (30, 3, 4) cube with no loop and no reshape. Each summary is then a single sum over an axis tuple: drop days and products to get one number per store, drop days and stores for one per product, drop stores and products for one per day. argmax returns a position, and indexing the products array with it turns that position into a name. The notebook follows with checks you should keep in your own work: revenue is finite everywhere, and store totals and daily totals reconcile to the same grand total.

The idea to remember

Name the axes.
Reconcile the totals.

A data pipeline is trustworthy when every stage keeps its shape, its missing mask and totals that agree however you sum them.

What makes the report trustworthy is discipline: knowing which axis is which, keeping the record of what was imputed, and checking that store, product and daily totals all reach the same 91119.66.
