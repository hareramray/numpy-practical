This chart draws the notebook's five points, x from 0 to 4 and y_data 1.1, 2.9, 5.2, 6.8 and 9.0, with the fitted line y = 1.06 + 1.97 x. The orange segments are the residuals, the vertical gaps between each point and the line. Least squares chooses the intercept and slope that make the sum of the squared gaps as small as possible; any other line would have a larger total. On the right, the design matrix shows why the column of ones matters: every row multiplies 1 by the intercept and x by the slope, so design @ coefficients is the prediction for every point at once. Try it: plot the observed points and the fitted line yourself, then change one y value to something extreme and refit. A single outlier pulls the line towards it, because squaring makes large gaps very expensive.
