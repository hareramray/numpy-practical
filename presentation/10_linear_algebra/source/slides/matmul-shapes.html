Step 1 · The shape rule

Inner sizes match, outer sizes stay

A (3, 2)

@

B (2, 4)

=

A @ B (3, 4)

(m, n) @ (n, p) → (m, p)

The inner sizes must agree (2 and 2). The outer sizes, 3 and 4, become the result's shape. The orange row times the blue column fills the one dark cell.

4

This is the one rule to check before any matrix product. For A shaped (m, n) and B shaped (n, p), the inner sizes n must match, because each row of A is paired element by element with each column of B. The result has the outer shape (m, p): one entry for every row of A and every column of B. In the diagram, the orange row of A and the blue column of B produce the single dark cell. On the right is the Try it answer: row 0 of A is [1, 2], column 0 of A is [1, 3], so the first entry is 1 times 1 plus 2 times 3, which is 7, matching the printed output. If the inner sizes disagree, NumPy raises a ValueError instead of guessing.
