Each bar is one column, so you can see the shapes. Projecting multiplies the 180 by 3 table by the transposed components, a 3 by 2 matrix whose columns are the two kept directions; the result, the orange embedding, gives every observation two coordinates, the ones plotted later. Going back multiplies by the 2 by 3 components and lands in the original feature space, drawn dashed because it is an approximation: the part of each point along the dropped third direction is gone. Because the directions are orthonormal, no inverse is needed: the transpose undoes the rotation. With all three directions the round trip is exact, which is Exercise 1.
