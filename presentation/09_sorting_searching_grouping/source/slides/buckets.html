This slide shows what bincount does with the codes. The top row is the five sales in their original order, each tagged with its code from np.unique: inverse is [2, 0, 2, 1, 0], because the sorted groups are coffee at 0, juice at 1 and tea at 2. Colour and label both name the group. bincount creates one slot per code and adds each sale's revenue into its slot: the two coffee sales, 20 and 30, land in slot 0 for 50; the single juice sale gives 12; the two tea sales, 10 and 15, give 25. Running bincount again without weights counts the sales per slot, 2, 1 and 2, and dividing gives the means 25.0, 12.0 and 12.5, matching the printed output. Because the buckets cover every sale exactly once, their totals add back up to the raw total of 87, which the notebook asserts.
