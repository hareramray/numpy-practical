Walk down each column. The first and third columns have nonzero units, so the mask is True there and np.divide writes 100 / 2 = 50 and 90 / 3 = 30 into the buffer. The middle column has zero units: the mask is False, the division is never attempted, and the cell keeps the NaN the buffer started with. That is the whole contract of out= plus where=: computed where allowed, untouched elsewhere. The card on the right is the notebook's Try it: if you start from zeros instead, the skipped cell silently claims a price of zero, which is a different and wrong statement about the data.
