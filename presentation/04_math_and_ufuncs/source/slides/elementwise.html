Step 1 · Elementwise calculations

Operators work element by element

quantity = np.array([2, 3, 1])
price = np.array([50., 20., 100.])
revenue = quantity * price
print("Revenue by product:", revenue)
print("Total:", revenue.sum())
print("Square roots:", np.sqrt([1., 4., 9.]))
np.testing.assert_allclose(
    np.log(np.exp([0., 1., 2.])), [0, 1, 2])

NumPy course · 04 Math and ufuncs

3

Arithmetic operators on two arrays of the same shape combine corresponding entries: 2 times 50, 3 times 20, 1 times 100, giving revenue [100, 60, 100] and a total of 260. Universal functions, or ufuncs, such as np.sqrt, np.exp and np.log, apply a function to every element and return an array of the same shape. The final assert shows that log undoes exp elementwise. Remember that * multiplies elementwise; matrix multiplication is the @ operator, covered later. Try it: apply a 10% discount with price * 0.9 before computing revenue. Revenue becomes [90, 54, 90] and the total drops from 260 to 234.
